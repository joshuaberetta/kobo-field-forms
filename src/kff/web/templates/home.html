Open your forms

Paper forms for your KoboToolbox projects

Print a deployed form as numbered sheets, fill them in with a pen where phones don't reach, then scan or photograph them. The answers are read on this computer, checked by a person where the reading is unsure, and sent to the same KoboToolbox project as every other submission.

Open your forms How it works

  1. Print

    Fetch a deployed form from KoboToolbox, pick the paper, languages and layout, and print as many sheets as you need. Each one has its own number.

  2. Fill in and scan

    People fill in bubbles, digits and text with a pen. Scan the sheets, or photograph them with a phone, in any order and any way up.

  3. Check and submit

    Every answer is read and given a confidence. You check the unsure ones next to the scan, then submit: each sheet becomes one submission.

How it works

Paper Forms turns a KoboToolbox form into sheets designed to be read by a computer: bubbles for choices, a box for each digit, lines for writing. Every page carries four corner markers and a QR code naming the form version, the sheet and the page. When a scan comes back, the markers straighten it and the QR code says exactly where every answer box is, so a crooked phone photo reads as well as a flat scan.

The answers are read on the computer this runs on. Choices and numbers need nothing else; handwritten text is read by a vision model if you set one up (see How answers are read). Anything unsure goes to a person, who sees the scanned answer next to the reading. Then each sheet is sent to KoboToolbox as one submission, against the form version it was printed from.

This is a proof of concept. It is tested end to end against KoboToolbox on synthetic sheets; real handwriting reads less well than the tests (see Limits).

Get started

  1. Start it. Run kff web and open this site's forms page. It runs on your own computer and only answers on it.
  2. Connect to KoboToolbox. Copy your API token from KoboToolbox (Account Settings → Security) into KOBO_TOKEN before starting kff web, and set KOBO_BASE_URL to global, eu or your server's address. The token is only needed to fetch forms and to submit; everything in between works without it, and without internet.
  3. Fetch a form. Paste a project's link or uid on the forms page. The deployed version is kept here, not unsaved edits, so the printed sheets match what KoboToolbox accepts.

Print sheets

Design sheets opens the designer: change any option and the preview shows one sheet as it will print. Answer areas from the layout map outlines every box the scanner will read. Generate PDF makes a batch of sheets, each with its own number in its QR codes and in the corner of every page.

Every question type becomes something a pen can answer:

In the formOn paper
Select one, select manyA bubble or square for each choice; short lists on one line, longer ones in columns.
Integer, decimal, rangeOne box per digit, as many as the question's constraint allows (. <= 120 gives three), and a printed decimal point.
Date, time, date and timeDigit boxes marked DD / MM / YYYY and HH : MM.
Location (geopoint)Latitude and longitude in digit boxes, with a sign box.
TextA row of letter boxes, or lines to write on (multi-line questions, and scripts like Arabic).
Signature, drawing, photoAn empty box; what's drawn in it is attached to the submission as an image.
Groups and repeatsHeaded sections; a repeat prints as numbered entries (its repeat count, or a number you choose).
NotesPrinted as text.
Calculations, hidden fields, start and endNot printed: nothing to fill in.
Audio, video, files, lines and shapes on a mapPrinted as "not collected on paper", and listed as a warning when you print.

Don't photocopy filled or blank sheets: copies share a sheet number, so only one of them could be submitted. Print more copies instead.

Print options

  • PaperA4, A5, A3, US Letter or US Legal, portrait or landscape.
  • Single- or double-sidedDouble-sided sheets get a binding margin, and a blank page when they'd end on a front, so a sheet never shares a leaf with the next one.
  • LanguagesOne language, or several: stacked under each other, side by side, or as separate sheets per language.
  • Question columnsOne or two columns of questions, where the page is wide enough.
  • Font sizeFrom 7 to 16 pt, for large print.
  • Choices and textChoices on one line, in columns or one per line; text in letter boxes or on lines.
  • RepeatsHow many entries a repeat without a fixed count gets.
  • What to showHints, skip logic, question numbers and the instructions box can each be left out.

The designer refuses layouts that can't work, such as two question columns on A5 or a choice list too long for one page, and says what to change.

Languages and scripts

Choose a page language and any others to print with it. The page language decides the direction of the page, the sheet's own words (instructions, "Answer only if…", page labels) and how text is written in. Arabic pages run right to left; numbers are still written left to right.

Latin and Arabic scripts are set up now, with fonts included, so sheets print the same everywhere and offline. Digit boxes accept Western digits and Eastern Arabic ones (٠١٢٣٤٥٦٧٨٩); both are sent to KoboToolbox as ordinary digits.

The sheet's own words exist in English, French, Spanish, Portuguese and Arabic. A language without them uses English, and you get a warning. More scripts can be added to the script settings (scripts.toml) with their fonts.

The non-English wording hasn't been checked by native speakers yet. Check a printed sheet before using it in the field.

Filling in

The instructions box on each sheet says it in short: a dark pen, bubbles filled in (a firm cross also reads), one letter or digit per box, and writing kept inside the boxes. Questions that skip logic would hide in KoboToolbox say so ("Answer only if Q3 is Yes"); answers to them are left out when the sheet is submitted.

Scanning

On the Scans page, upload scanned PDFs or photos: many pages at once, in any order, sideways or upside down. 200 dpi is plenty; a phone photo of the whole page, flat and in focus, works too. Each page is found by its markers and QR code, straightened, and shown with every answer area drawn on it, so you can see it landed right. The page also lists sheets with pages still missing.

A scan that can't be read says why: a marker cut off, a blurred QR code, a sheet printed from another computer's store. Scanning the same page again replaces the earlier scan.

How answers are read

  • Choices by how much of each bubble is inked. A faint mark, or two marked where one is expected, is flagged.
  • Digits by a small model that runs on this computer, trained on real handwritten digits. A digit that could be another, a gap between digits, or a date that doesn't exist is flagged.
  • Handwritten text by a vision model, if one is set up: any service with an OpenAI-compatible API, such as a model running on the same machine (KFF_VLM_URL). Without one, written text is flagged for you to type in; it is never guessed.
  • Signatures and drawings are attached to the submission as an image of the box.

Each reading gets a confidence, and anything under the bar goes to review.

Review and submit

Review & submit lists the scanned sheets: pages found, answers read, how many to check, and whether they're in KoboToolbox yet. A sheet's page shows every question with its scanned answer beside the reading, the ones to check first.

  • Correct an answer, or tick checked to confirm it. Checked answers are kept if the sheet is read again.
  • Missing required answers and answers that break the question's rules are listed too.
  • Submit to KoboToolbox sends the sheet as one submission, against the form version it was printed from, with the sheet number as its id. Submitting a sheet twice can't make a second record: KoboToolbox recognises it as a duplicate.

Working offline

After a form is fetched, printing, scanning, reading and review need no internet. Submitting needs the KoboToolbox server: KoboToolbox online, or a local one, such as a Kobo in a box field laptop serving KoboToolbox on its own network, which can also run the vision model for handwriting. Point KOBO_BASE_URL at it; a local server over plain http needs KOBO_ALLOW_HTTP=true.

Everything lives in one folder (KFF_HOME): forms, printed sheets, scans and readings. Copy it to move the work to another computer; a sheet can only be read where its batch was printed, or from a copy of that folder.

Privacy and security

  • Runs on your computer. This site only answers on the computer it runs on, and refuses requests made through other addresses, so another website can't reach it.
  • Your token stays put. The KoboToolbox token is read when kff web starts and is only ever sent to your KoboToolbox server. The browser never sees it.
  • Scans stay here. Scanned pages and readings are kept in KFF_HOME. Crops of written answers go to the vision model you configure, and nowhere else; with none configured, nothing leaves the computer except the submissions.
  • Nothing is deleted from KoboToolbox. The only thing it removes there is the throwaway kff-e2e- test project the end-to-end test makes for itself.

Limits

  • Real handwriting reads worse than the tests. The tests write answers in handwriting-style fonts; real hands vary more. Digit reading is trained on real handwritten Western digits; Eastern Arabic digits are trained on printed shapes only so far, and are flagged more often.
  • Text needs a vision model, and how well it reads handwriting hasn't been measured on real sheets yet.
  • Skip logic is printed in words for the usual rules (equals, comparisons, selected, and/or); unusual expressions are printed with their question numbers, and if they can't be worked out the answer is kept.
  • Not on paper: audio, video, file uploads, and lines and shapes on a map; ranking and matrix questions; choice lists from a file.
  • One store per batch: sheets are read with the store they were printed from (see Working offline).

Help

A page won't scan

All four corner markers and the QR code must be in the picture, and the page flat. Rescan at 200 dpi or more, or take the photo straight on in good light. The error says which part was missing.

"Isn't in this store"

The sheet was printed from another computer, or from another KFF_HOME folder. Read it there, or copy that folder here.

The form changed after printing

Sheets keep the form version they were printed from, and are submitted against it. Print new sheets for the new version; KoboToolbox shows submissions from both.

Can I test the whole thing without people filling in sheets?

Yes: kff e2e makes a throwaway test project in KoboToolbox, fills in synthetic sheets, scans, reads and submits them, checks what KoboToolbox stored, and deletes the project. kff e2e --offline does it without KoboToolbox.

Where do I get help with KoboToolbox itself?

The KoboToolbox Help Center covers forms, projects and data, and the community forum is the place for questions.
